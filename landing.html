01

Motivation & background

Deep neural networks achieve remarkable performance across vision, language, and multimodal tasks — yet their internal representations remain largely opaque.

Standard evaluation metrics (accuracy, precision, recall) measure task performance but provide no insight into the reasons behind a model's output. This lack of transparency poses risks in high-stakes domains: medical diagnosis, autonomous vehicles, credit scoring, and legal decision support.

Concept-based interpretability methods address this gap by measuring how strongly a model's predictions correlate with human-interpretable concepts — stripes, texture, color, shape — without requiring access to the model's training data or internal weights.

02

What is TCAV?

Testing with Concept Activation Vectors (TCAV) quantifies the importance of a user-defined concept to a model's predictions. The core idea is elegant: collect examples of a concept (striped images) and non-examples, train a binary linear classifier in the model's activation space, and measure how often changes in the concept direction affect predictions.

The TCAV score for a concept C with respect to class k is defined as:

$$ \text{TCAV}_{C,k} = \frac{|\{ x \in X_k : S_{C,k}(x) > 0 \}|}{|X_k|} $$

where SC,k(x) measures the directional derivative of the model's prediction along the concept activation vector at layer l.

03

Research objectives

Three core questions guide our experimental work across TCAV, Visual-TCAV, and related attribution frameworks.

  • Sensitivity analysis — How robust are concept attribution scores to choice of layer, concept definition, and model architecture?
  • Comparative evaluation — How does TCAV's concept attribution compare with gradient-based and perturbation-based attribution methods in terms of consistency and interpretability?
  • Visual extension — Can Visual-TCAV's per-pixel concept attributions provide more actionable insights than global TCAV scores while maintaining statistical reliability?

04

Methodology overview

Concept dataset construction

Curating balanced sets of concept-positive and concept-negative examples from standard vision datasets, with careful controls for confounding factors.

Activation space analysis

Training CAVs at multiple network depths to evaluate how concept information is represented hierarchically across early, middle, and late layers.

Statistical validation

Computing TCAV scores across multiple random seeds, concept splits, and model checkpoints to assess statistical significance and reproducibility.
