Chapter 2 · State of the art

Concept-Based Explainability Methods

A review of the current landscape of concept-based explainability, placing TCAV and Visual-TCAV within a taxonomy that spans supervised concept methods, unsupervised concept discovery, concept-structured architectures and causal concept reasoning.

Explore methods Positioning

Methods reviewed in this chapter

01

Mapping the field

The review follows a logical progression of the field: from the shortcomings of saliency methods that motivated the turn toward concepts, to the supervised methods at the core of this work, to the unsupervised and hybrid approaches that address the scalability of user-defined concepts.

The supervised concept-based school of thought begins with the premise that a human analyst can define a concept of interest by providing a set of example images, and that the model’s internal representations can be probed to determine whether and how that concept influences predictions. Other lines of work discover concepts automatically, build them into the architecture, or reason about them causally.

Supervised concepts

Concepts are defined by the user through example images. TCAV, Regression Concept Vectors, Network Dissection and Visual-TCAV.

Automatic extraction

Concepts are discovered from the network’s own activations. ACE, CRAFT and ConceptSHAP.

Beyond post-hoc CAVs

Concept Bottleneck Models, the Causal Concept Effect and Concept Relevance Propagation.

02

Supervised concept methods

From Concept Activation Vectors to Visual-TCAV: a human analyst defines a concept with example images, and the model’s internal representations are probed to determine whether and how that concept influences predictions.

TCAV · Kim et al. 2018

Foundation

Concept Activation Vectors

Kim et al. introduced the foundational framework for supervised concept-based explainability. To compute a CAV, a user provides a set of examples illustrating a concept, alongside a set of random counterexamples. The network processes these inputs to extract their representations at a chosen internal layer, and a linear classifier is trained to separate the concept’s activations from the random ones. The CAV is the vector normal to this separating hyperplane.

Formally, given a positive set $C_p$ and a negative set $C_n$, the CAV $v_C^l \in \mathbb{R}^m$ is the weight vector of a binary linear classifier trained to distinguish $\{f_l(x) : x \in C_p\}$ from $\{f_l(x) : x \in C_n\}$, where $f_l$ maps the input to the $m$ activations of layer $l$. Writing $w$ for the returned weight vector, the CAV is taken at unit length:

$$ \hat{v}_C^l = \frac{w}{\lVert w \rVert_2} $$

For a convolutional layer the activation is a tensor $f_l(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$. It may be flattened into $\mathbb{R}^{C \cdot H \cdot W}$, or reduced by global average pooling to a channel-wise vector, which discards where each channel responds but lowers the dimensionality by a factor of $HW$:

$$ \bar{a}_c = \frac{1}{HW} \sum_{h,w} f_l(x)_{c,h,w} $$
TCAV · Kim et al. 2018

Directional derivatives

Conceptual sensitivity and the TCAV score

The conceptual sensitivity of class $k$ to concept $C$ for an input $x$ is the directional derivative of the class logit $h_{l,k}$ with respect to the layer activations $f_l(x)$, in the direction of the CAV:

$$ S_{C,k,l}(x) = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \frac{h_{l,k}\big(f_l(x) + \epsilon\, v_C^l\big) - h_{l,k}\big(f_l(x)\big)}{\epsilon} = \nabla h_{l,k}\big(f_l(x)\big) \cdot v_C^l $$

The global TCAV score is the fraction of class $k$ inputs whose layer $l$ activations are positively influenced by concept $C$:

$$ \mathrm{TCAVQ}_{C,k,l} = \frac{\big|\{x \in X_k : S_{C,k,l}(x) > 0\}\big|}{|X_k|} $$

TCAV requires little machine learning expertise, adapts to any concept a user can define through examples, works without retraining the network and produces a single metric for entire classes. Because the score relies only on the sign of $S_{C,k,l}$, it discards magnitude, and because it is computed on whole inputs it lacks spatial granularity.

RCV · Graziani et al. 2018

Linear regressor in place of
the binary concept classifier

Continuous concepts

Regression Concept Vectors

Graziani et al. extended the CAV framework with Regression Concept Vectors, which replace the binary concept classifier with a linear regressor. Concepts can then be defined along a continuous scale rather than as a binary presence or absence, which is useful where concepts are quantitative, such as the degree of cell irregularity in histopathology or the intensity of a colour.

The directional derivative along an RCV quantifies how sensitive the prediction is to a continuous increase or decrease in the concept’s magnitude. The extension still operates at the global level without spatial grounding.

Network Dissection · Bau et al. 2017

IoU between unit activation maps
and concept annotation masks

A complementary perspective

Network Dissection

Network Dissection evaluates whether individual neurons or feature maps align with semantically meaningful categories, a foundational example of mechanistic explainability. A densely annotated corpus, the Broden dataset, is passed through the network, and the intersection-over-union between each unit’s activation map and each concept’s mask is measured. A unit is a detector for a concept if the IoU exceeds a threshold.

Where TCAV asks “is this concept important for this prediction?”, Network Dissection asks “does any unit in this layer encode this concept?”. A concept may be encoded without being used for the prediction, or influence predictions through distributed representations that no single unit detects.

Visual-TCAV · De Santis et al. 2024

Spatial grounding

Visual-TCAV and the pooled-CAV

De Santis et al. introduced Visual-TCAV to address two limitations of TCAV: the absence of spatial information and the inability to quantify per-instance concept importance. For each input image it produces a concept map that localises where the network recognises a concept, and a concept attribution score that estimates how much that concept contributes to the prediction of a class.

Instead of a linear classifier, Visual-TCAV computes the CAV by Difference of Means, a parameter-free and deterministic estimator recommended by Martin and Weller:

$$ \mu_p = \frac{1}{|C_p|} \sum_{x \in C_p} f_l(x), \qquad \mu_n = \frac{1}{|C_n|} \sum_{x \in C_n} f_l(x), \qquad \hat{v}_C^l = \frac{\mu_p - \mu_n}{\lVert \mu_p - \mu_n \rVert_2} $$

Its failure mode: the centroid difference is only as faithful as the concept signal is dominant over any other systematic difference between the two pools. When another difference dominates, the direction aligns with that axis rather than with the concept. A Global Average Pooling of the CAV then yields the pooled-CAV, whose length equals the number of feature maps in the target layer.

Concept map

Where the concept is recognised

The concept map is a weighted sum of the feature maps at the target layer, using the pooled-CAV values $p_k^c$ as weights, followed by a ReLU that retains only positively correlated regions:

$$ M^c = \mathrm{ReLU}\Big( \sum_k p_k^c \cdot \mathrm{fmaps}_k \Big) $$

This is structurally analogous to Grad-CAM, with one distinction: Grad-CAM weights the maps with the global-average-pooled gradients of the class logit, producing a class-discriminative map, while Visual-TCAV uses the pooled-CAV, producing a concept-discriminative map. The concept map is therefore class-agnostic: it shows where the network recognises a concept regardless of which class is being predicted.

Concept attribution

How much the concept contributes

The attribution is a generalised application of Integrated Gradients in feature-map space, integrating from a zero baseline $F_0$ to the feature maps $F$ along a straight line, where $z_k$ is the logit for class $k$:

$$ \mathrm{IG}^k = (F - F_0) \odot \int_0^1 \frac{\partial z_k\big(F_0 + \alpha (F - F_0)\big)}{\partial F}\, d\alpha $$

These attributions are normalised, weighted by the pooled-CAV and spatially masked by the concept map to give the concept attribution score:

$$ a_{c,k} = \sum_{i,j} M_{ij}^{c,\mathrm{norm}} \cdot \Big( \sum_k \hat{p}_k^c \cdot \widehat{\mathrm{IG}}{}^k \Big)_{ij} $$

The concept map only answers whether and where a pattern was detected. By intersecting it with the class-specific gradients, $a_{c,k}$ isolates the features that were both recognised as the concept and used to increase the logit for class $k$. Visual-TCAV was designed for spatial grounding (D1), per-instance attribution (D2) and aggregatability (D3), so local attributions can be averaged into class-level explanations comparable to TCAV scores.

03

Automatic concept extraction

Supervised methods require concepts to be defined in advance. That requirement is natural where a vocabulary already exists, such as the 7-point checklist in dermoscopy, but becomes a bottleneck when the goal is to discover what concepts a network has learned.

ACE · Ghorbani et al. 2019

Multi-resolution segments,
k-means clusters as concepts

Automatic discovery

ACE: Automatic Concept-based Explanations

ACE segments training images at multiple resolutions, extracts the activations of each segment and clusters them with k-means. Each cluster is interpreted as a concept, and its importance is estimated with TCAV scores computed from CAVs derived from the cluster centroids.

ACE removes manual concept definition but the discovered “concepts” are not guaranteed to be semantically meaningful, the number of clusters must be chosen, and the segmentation resolution heavily influences what is found. The main reason to prefer TCAV is semantic control: manually defined concepts keep the explanation grounded in the user’s exact vocabulary.

CRAFT · Fel et al. 2023

Non-negative factorization

CRAFT

CRAFT applies Non-negative Matrix Factorization to the activation tensor, decomposing it into non-negative concept components, an inductive bias that encourages parts-based, interpretable decompositions. It adds a recursive strategy that detects concepts across multiple layers, Sobol-index-based concept importance and Concept Attribution Maps obtained through implicit differentiation.

Zhang et al. use the same connection in Non-negative Concept Activation Vectors (NCAVs), whose framework, Invertible Concept-based Explanations (ICE), outperformed standard CAVs in fidelity and interpretability. CRAFT still requires the number of components to be fixed in advance, and its factors need human labelling before they can be regarded as concepts.

ConceptSHAP · Yeh et al. 2020

Shapley values over concept sets,
optimised for completeness

Completeness

ConceptSHAP

Yeh et al. introduced completeness for concept-based explanations: the set of concepts should be sufficient to account for the model’s prediction behaviour. ConceptSHAP discovers concepts by optimising for completeness while encouraging interpretability, and estimates importance with Shapley values over concept sets, capturing interactions that TCAV, which evaluates each concept independently, cannot.

Computing Shapley values is exponential in the number of concepts and must be approximated. Completeness guarantees that the set is predictive, not that its members are individually interpretable, and the number of concepts remains a free parameter.

04

Architectures, causality and relevance

Three further lines of work: concepts built into the prediction pipeline, causal rather than correlational concept effects, and relevance propagation conditioned on concepts.

CBM · Koh et al. 2020

Ante-hoc concepts

Concept Bottleneck Models

In a Concept Bottleneck Model the network first predicts a set of human-defined concept labels from the input and then predicts the class label from those concepts. All information passes through this bottleneck, so concept predictions are inspectable and intervenable: a user can correct a mispredicted concept at test time and observe how the correction propagates.

CBMs require dense concept annotations at training time and lose accuracy if the annotated concepts do not capture all relevant information. Mahinpei et al. showed that CBM concept representations can encode information beyond the predefined concepts, which they term information leakage.

CaCE · Goyal et al. 2019

VAE-generated images with and
without the concept

Causal reasoning

Causal Concept Effect

The methods above establish correlational relationships: a high TCAV score shows that a class’s predictions correlate with the concept direction, not that the concept causes them. The Causal Concept Effect measures the causal effect of the presence or absence of a concept, using a Variational Autoencoder to generate images with and without the concept and measuring the change in output.

If a concept correlates with a class only because it co-occurs with other discriminative features, TCAV will attribute importance to it, while CaCE can, in principle, separate genuine causal influence from spurious correlation.

CRP · Achtibat et al. 2023

Layer-wise Relevance Propagation
conditioned on concept subspaces

Local and global

Concept Relevance Propagation

CRP combines the local perspective of attribution with the global perspective of concepts, answering both “where” and “what” for individual predictions. It propagates relevance with Layer-wise Relevance Propagation, conditioned on concept subspaces at intermediate layers, producing per-prediction concept relevance maps, concept atlases and concept-composition analyses.

CRP requires propagation rules suited to each architecture and layer type, and does not yield a single scalar importance score. It occupies a similar position to Visual-TCAV, and a comparative evaluation between the two remains an open research question.

05

Positioning of this work

The landscape can be organised by whether concepts are user-defined or discovered, local or global, post-hoc or ante-hoc, and correlational or causal.

For dermoscopic skin lesion classification, this taxonomy points to a single natural quadrant. Dermoscopy already has a clinically validated concept vocabulary in the 7-point checklist, which makes supervised concept definition natural and removes the need for the unsupervised discovery of ACE, CRAFT or ConceptSHAP. The post-hoc paradigm allows existing high-performance classifiers to be analysed without retraining, unlike the ante-hoc commitment of Concept Bottleneck Models.

Within that quadrant, Visual-TCAV supplies the spatial grounding the clinical setting demands: a dermatologist does not only assess whether a pigment network is present, but also its location, extent and regularity within the lesion boundary.
