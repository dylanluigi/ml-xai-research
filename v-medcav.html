Chapter 4 · V-MedCAV

TCAV and Visual-TCAV on a Real Dermatological Classifier

The configuration validated on synthetic data is transferred to a fine-tuned Inception-V4 classifier for melanoma, melanocytic nevus and seborrheic keratosis, probed with a seventeen-concept dermoscopic vocabulary grounded in clinician-curated image sets.

01

From synthetic to clinical

Does the reliability established under synthetic ground truth carry over to a clinical classifier whose true sensitivity to each concept can never be directly observed?

The synthetic phase showed that Visual-TCAV’s concept maps can be trusted to localise the concepts they claim to. That licenses, but does not by itself substantiate, the claim that the same method applied to a real diagnostic classifier produces explanations that are clinically meaningful. The transition introduces three asymmetries.

No per-pixel masks

Concepts such as the pigment network or the blue-whitish veil have no comparable spatial ground truth; their extent is itself a matter of clinical judgement.

No single-concept supervision

Each of the three diagnostic classes is characterised by a constellation of concepts whose relative importance must be discovered by the explanation method.

A clinical evaluation axis

The question is whether the explanation agrees with the diagnostic reasoning documented in the dermoscopy literature, not whether it recovers a known function.

  • Determine whether TCAV and Visual-TCAV, under the matched-absent configuration, recover class-specific concept profiles that agree with established dermoscopic knowledge.
  • Assess whether the spatial concept maps localise in dermoscopically plausible lesion regions.
  • Corroborate the recovered profiles against an independent annotation by an expert dermatologist.

02

Dataset and target model

The task is the three-class problem over $\{\mathrm{Mel}, \mathrm{Nev}, \mathrm{Sk}\}$: melanoma, melanocytic nevus and seborrheic keratosis, the canonical malignant, benign-melanocytic and benign-non-melanocytic triad. The corpus unifies ISIC 2019, BCN20000 (Hospital Clínic of Barcelona) and HAM10000, which contributes the seborrheic-keratosis-rich subset.

Preprocessing follows Menegola et al.: each RGB image is divided by 255 and its own per-channel mean is subtracted, with no division by the standard deviation. The same normalisation is applied in training, concept activations and attribution.

Inception-V4

An ImageNet-pretrained Inception-V4 whose head is replaced by dropout (0.5) and a fully connected layer projecting the 1536-dimensional feature vector onto the three classes. The backbone has twenty-two feature blocks; the deepest yields 1536 channels at $8 \times 8$ for a $299 \times 299$ input. The probing targets are the three terminal InceptionC blocks, the shallow, intermediate and deepest probing blocks, the deepest coinciding with the bottleneck.

03

Dermoscopic concept vocabulary

The seventeen-concept set of Lucieri et al., derived from the seven-point checklist and the PH2 annotations, covers morphological structures, global symmetry and chromatic complexity. Nine concepts raise the suspicion of melanoma, five correspond to benign melanocytic lesions. The polarity is not used in training; it is the reference for interpreting the results.

For each concept, positives and concept-absent negatives are drawn from the same archive (derm7pt or PH2). This is the decisive choice: negatives from a different dataset would make the CAV separate acquisition device, illumination and framing rather than the clinical concept.

Clinical ground truth was built with the Dermatology Unit of Son Llàtzer Hospital (Palma), with annotations by Dr. Rosa Taberner, who recorded the presence and location of each concept on held-out test images.

04

Configuration and protocol

V-MedCAV keeps the Visual-TCAV core and substitutes the AIXI-Shape library for the clinician-curated concept sets. The CAV uses the centroid construction with per-concept concept-absent pools; the classifier construction is kept as the pool-robust alternative.

150

maximum positive images per concept, with absent pools up to 300

20

independent CAVs per concept and block, each from a fresh resampling

3

probing blocks evaluated jointly

156

test images: 53 melanoma, 52 nevus, 51 seborrheic keratosis

The fourteen derm7pt concepts reach the 300 ceiling, while the three PH2 concepts are bounded by the smaller cohort: 117 positives for ASYM, 148 for ASYM_2AX and 161 for COL3. The directional analysis follows Lucieri et al., recording for each concept, block and class the mean and standard deviation of the sign count and the held-out CAV accuracy. The spatial analysis aggregates Visual-TCAV attribution per class with a $2\sigma$ confidence interval, and the clinical analysis compares the model’s per-lesion concept calls with Dr. Taberner’s annotations.

05

Scalar attribution with TCAV

Experiment 1: the TCAV sign count per concept and target class at the deepest probing block, with the clinical polarity as the prior.

Melanoma

Nine melanoma-indicative concepts, BWV, ST_IR, VS_IR, PIG_IR, RS_BA, RS_CO, ASYM, ASYM_2AX and COL3, saturate at exactly 1.000, while the benign markers DG_R and PIG_R collapse to 0.000.

Nevus

The polarity inverts with only the target class changed: DG_R (1.000), PIG_R (1.000) and PN_T (0.800) move to the top, while BWV, VS_IR, RS_CO, ASYM, ASYM_2AX and COL3 are driven to 0.000.

Seborrheic keratosis

No coherent polarity. Its highest counts (RS_WA and ASYM_2AX at 1.000) form no diagnostic group; it blends the two melanocytic profiles, as Lucieri et al. also found.

At the bottleneck the melanoma-indicative concepts saturate at 1.000 with zero variance across the resampled CAVs, so the verdict is unambiguous but its magnitude is capped. The conclusions therefore read the sign counts jointly with the continuous Visual-TCAV attributions.

06

Spatial attribution with Visual-TCAV

Experiments 2 and 3: local maps for representative test cases, and global attributions aggregated over the test images of each class with $2\sigma$ confidence intervals.

At the deepest block the ranking is led by COL3 at mean 0.237 $[0.233, 0.241]$, ASYM_2AX at 0.172 $[0.168, 0.176]$ and ASYM at 0.123 $[0.119, 0.128]$, followed by RS_CO at 0.080 $[0.075, 0.086]$. Every benign concept lies at or below $10^{-3}$. The total mass rises from 0.03 at the shallowest block and 0.11 at the intermediate block to 0.68 at the deepest.

The four highest means are DG_R at 0.116 $[0.110, 0.123]$, PIG_R at 0.101 $[0.095, 0.108]$, ST_R at 0.097 $[0.090, 0.104]$ and PN_T at 0.075 $[0.070, 0.081]$, all benign indicators. Every melanoma-indicative concept is at or below $10^{-3}$: the attribution inverts completely, from the same CAVs, by changing only the target class.

The largest values are COL3 at 0.005 $[0.004, 0.006]$ and ASYM_2AX at 0.003 $[0.002, 0.004]$. The total mass, 0.009, is roughly seventy times smaller than the melanoma mass of 0.676. Milia-like cysts, comedo-like openings, fissures and ridges and the cerebriform surface are not in the vocabulary: a vocabulary gap rather than a model deficit.

Where the maps activate, they localise over the pigmented body of the lesion rather than the surrounding skin, at the deepest block, and with selectivity by polarity. In the absence of per-pixel masks, spatial fidelity here is a qualitative judgement bounded by the global evidence.

07

Cross-method convergence

Experiment 4: TCAV reports a frequency of positive influence, Visual-TCAV a spatial attribution magnitude. Do they agree on the ranking at the decision layer?

Over all 153 combinations of class, block and concept the two correlate only weakly (Spearman $\rho = 0.18$), which is expected. At the bottleneck, the concepts with the most attribution are also those with a saturated sign count: COL3, ASYM_2AX, ASYM and RS_CO for melanoma; DG_R, PIG_R and PN_T for nevus. Some concepts reach a sign count of one with almost no attribution (RS_BA and PIG_IR for melanoma).

Rank agreement is moderate for melanoma ($\rho = 0.52$) and nevus ($\rho = 0.56$) and weak for seborrheic keratosis ($\rho = 0.19$). Across all three classes the methods share three to four of their top five concepts, always from the clinically correct family.

08

Clinical validation

Experiment 5: concept-level agreement between the model and an expert dermatologist.

Dr. Rosa Taberner annotated six held-out lesions, three melanomas, two melanocytic nevi and one seborrheic keratosis, with the presence or absence of every concept in Table 4.2 and the regions where it was observed. Her annotation is the reference; the model calls a concept present when its Visual-TCAV local attribution is strictly positive.

Across the 63 concept-level comparisons the model agrees with the expert on close to four fifths of decisions. A recall of 0.739 shows the model recovers most concepts the dermatologist marks as present, and a precision of 0.680 shows most concepts it calls present are endorsed by the clinician. The cohort is deliberately compact, a proof of feasibility rather than a statistically powered trial.

09

V-MedCAV conclusions

The central finding is a clean separation between the two melanocytic classes.

At the deepest block the melanoma decision is dominated by the chromatic, asymmetry and regression concepts (COL3, ASYM_2AX, ASYM, RS_CO), while the nevus decision is dominated by the regular-pattern subgroup (DG_R, PIG_R, ST_R, PN_T). The attribution inverts almost completely, from the same CAVs, by changing only the target class, and both profiles match the dermatological literature. That this dissociation emerges from a network trained on diagnostic labels alone is a non-trivial confirmation that it has internalised distinctions aligned with dermoscopic reasoning.

The second finding is a negative result. The seborrheic-keratosis class produces almost no attribution, an accurate report of a vocabulary gap: its distinguishing features are not part of the derm7pt or PH2 schemas. The melanocytic results are confirmed by the standard TCAV sign counts and, most directly, by the clinical annotation (accuracy 0.778).

10

Limitations and future directions

Concept-based explainability offers a reliable bridge between a model’s representations and expert reasoning, but a conditional one, trustworthy to the extent that the negative pool, the probing depth and the concept vocabulary are chosen with care.

Limitations

  • Without per-pixel ground truth, spatial fidelity rests on qualitative inspection checked by a compact expert annotation.
  • The concept set is bounded by the two clinical schemas and cannot expose behaviour governed by concepts outside them.
  • Concept and test images share the dermoscopic acquisition distribution, so the explanations should not be transferred to other modalities without further validation.
  • Rarer concepts are estimated from smaller, noisier pools, and the global attribution is a measure of directional alignment, not a causal quantification.
  • The findings were established on sequential and Inception-based CNNs; Vision Transformers and heavily residual networks remain to be verified.

Future directions

  • A magnitude-based directional derivative, as in the synthetic phase, to grade the bottleneck concepts that currently saturate the sign count.
  • Extending the vocabulary with keratosis-specific features such as milia-like cysts and comedo-like openings.
  • A completeness measure such as ConceptSHAP, to separate a vocabulary gap from a true model deficit.
  • Moving toward causal concept reasoning, in the spirit of the causal and propagation-based methods of Chapter 2, to distinguish concepts the model uses from those it merely co-detects.
